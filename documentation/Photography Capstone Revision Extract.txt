Validated User-Centered Problem Statement


         For  Jonathan    Photography, maintaining         clear   communication       with prospective clients is

essential   to   delivering    reliable   and    personalized      photography      and    videography      services.

However,     the   studio’s   existing   inquiry   and    booking    process    depends      largely   on   referrals,

Facebook     Messenger,      personal     conversations,      handwritten      records,   and    manual     calendar

checking. During the interview, the owner explained, “Clients usually contact us through chat or

personal   conversations.” He further stated, “The information clients usually ask about includes

the  available   packages      and   their  corresponding      prices.”   Although    these    practices   allow   the

owner     to  communicate        personally     with   clients,   important     information—including         service

preferences,    estimated     prices,  event dates,     coverage times,      locations, and contact       details—is

distributed across separate conversations and physical records.


         As  presented     in Figure   1, the   researchers     conducted a direct interview and           prototype

evaluation    with  the   owner    of  Jonathan    Photography. The session allowed the              researchers    to

examine how the        studio receives client      inquiries,  discusses    package    prices, presents previous

work,   verifies   event    schedules,    and    records    booking    information.     When     asked    about    the

information collected from clients, the owner explained, “Before accepting a booking, I need to

know the     event date,    time, location, preferred       package, and the       client’s contact information.”

This  demonstrates       that  several   important     details  must    be  gathered     and   verified  before    the

owner can properly evaluate a booking request.

         The   existing  process presents several challenges            for the   owner. Schedule availability is

manually checked using a calendar and traditional records. The owner stated, “I currently use a

calendar   and record book to monitor my schedules and client transactions.” Previous inquiries

and   booking     information     may    also   need    to  be   retrieved   from    separate    conversations      or

handwritten notes. As the number of inquiries increases, this method can make it more difficult

to determine     which    clients  are   still inquiring,  which   have   submitted     booking    requests, which

schedules    have    been    confirmed,     and   which    transactions     have   already    been    completed     or

cancelled.


         The   interview   also  revealed    that  service prices    are not always fixed because they may

depend on the nature of the event, the requested coverage, available equipment, and additional

client requirements. According to the owner, “Package prices may still be adjusted depending on



=== PAGE 9 ===

the client’s  needs,    the event    requirements,     and the final agreement.” This explains why direct

communication must remain part of the booking process. An online estimate can provide clients

with  an   initial idea  of  the   expected    cost,  but   it cannot    replace   the   consultation required to

determine the final package, payment arrangement, and other event-specific conditions.


         Despite   the importance of       direct  consultation, the      owner   recognized     the usefulness     of

allowing   clients  to  review    services   and   prepare    a preliminary estimate before contacting the

studio. He stated, “An online estimator would be a great help because clients could already have

an  idea   of  the   possible   price  before    we   discuss   the  final  arrangement.”      This   would   reduce

repetitive questions about basic packages and prices while allowing personal communication to

focus  on   the specific requirements of the event. The owner also identified the need to include

event   time,   equipment      upgrades,     video   portfolio   content,    payment     agreements,      and   more

accessible booking records.


         Following    the Point of    View   method,    the researchers met an independent photography

studio owner who personally manages client inquiries, package explanations, price adjustments,

schedule    checking,    website    content, and booking confirmations. The owner explained,                   “I still

prefer direct communication with clients because some packages and additional fees need to be

adjusted    through    consultation.”    This   preference     indicates   that   the  studio   does   not   need   to

eliminate   personal    communication.       Instead, it needs      a more    organized    method of      supporting

those conversations and preserving the information produced through them.


         The   researchers    were surprised to discover that important client and event information

is managed      across    online   messages,      personal    conversations,      a  calendar,    and   handwritten

records. The     owner    stated,  “I prefer having      both   personal   and   digitized   records because it is

easier to   check the details when they are properly organized.” This suggests that the principal



=== PAGE 10 ===

difficulty  is not   the  owner’s    willingness    to  communicate       with   clients,  but   the  absence    of  a

consistent and centralized process for recording, retrieving, and monitoring information between

conversations.


         The   researchers     therefore   wondered      whether    the repeated      clarification, manual     work,

and possible scheduling difficulties         experienced by the studio could be reduced by providing a

more    structured     method     of   managing      inquiries    and    booking     information.     It  would     be

game-changing to allow prospective clients to independently review services, examine portfolio

work, and prepare a preliminary estimate while giving the owner a dependable way to monitor

client  details,   requested      schedules,     booking    statuses,     and   service    information.     Such    an

approach     would     support    the   owner’s     existing    workflow     without     removing     the   personal

consultation required before a booking becomes final.


         Based    on   the   interview    findings,   the   validated    user-centered     problem     is  stated   as

follows:  The owner      and   prospective    clients of Jonathan Photography need a clearer and more

consistent     way    to    organize,     access,    and     communicate        service,    pricing,    client,   and

event-schedule information during the inquiry and booking process because the current reliance

on   separate    online    conversations,     personal     discussions,    handwritten      records,   and    manual

schedule    checking     makes     information     difficult  to   track   and   retrieve,    potentially   affecting

response time, record accuracy, productivity, and service consistency.

Evidence Supporting the Problem


         The evidence supporting the identified problem was gathered through the interview and

prototype    evaluation    conducted     with   the   owner    of  Jonathan     Photography.      The   researchers

examined the      studio’s  existing   inquiry and booking       process to understand what information is

requested     from   clients,   how    the   information     is communicated        and    recorded,    and   where

repeated    clarification   and   manual    work    occur.  The   interview    showed     that  clients  commonly

discover   the  studio   through    referrals, Facebook,      or previous customers        before   contacting    the

owner    through    Messenger      or  personal    conversations.     The   owner    explained,    “Clients   usually

contact us through chat or personal conversations, and they commonly ask about the available

packages     and   prices.”   This   means     that  the   owner    becomes     directly   involved    even   at  the

beginning of the inquiry, particularly when the client is only looking for basic information about

services and pricing.


         At the concrete or “What”         level, the process begins when a prospective client contacts

the studio and asks about its services. The owner then gathers information needed to evaluate

the request,    including   the client’s   name,    address, contact number, budget, preferred package,

event date, time, and location. The owner stated, “Before accepting a booking, I need to know

the client’s  name,    address, contact      number,    budget, preferred package, event date, time, and

location.”  The   interview   notes also indicate      that package selection may depend on the client’s

budget,    meaning     that  the   owner    must    consider    both   the  client’s  available   budget    and   the

requirements of the event when discussing a suitable package. This shows that a single inquiry

involves   several   pieces   of  information    that  must    be  collected   before   the owner can properly

evaluate the request.



=== PAGE 12 ===

The    interview     also   showed      that   portfolio    presentation      is  part   of   the   personal

communication       process.    The   owner    presents    previous    work   and    may   directly   send   portfolio

samples    to  prospective     clients   while   explaining    the  available    photography      and   videography

services.   This  means that      clients may need to       request    or  receive   examples of     previous work

through direct communication before deciding whether the studio’s services match their needs.

This  adds   another    part of   the inquiry    process that     is currently   handled     manually rather than

through one organized source of information.


         At  the  “How”     level,  the  activities  are   completed     through    separate    conversations      and

manual records. Package explanations, price discussions, portfolio samples, schedule availability,

payment     arrangements,        and    other   requirements       may    be    discussed     through    Messenger,

telephone calls, or personal meetings. The owner stated, “I currently use a calendar and record

book to monitor my schedules and client transactions.” The interview notes further indicate that

the owner prefers having both personal and digitized records because organized records make it

easier to   check details.    Because     communication       records   and schedule      records are maintained

separately, information      must still be manually        transferred, remembered, or          retrieved when an

inquiry progresses toward a booking.


         The   interview    also   revealed    that  pricing   is  not   always    fixed.  The   owner     explained,

“Package prices may be adjusted depending on the client’s needs, the event requirements, and

the  final  agreement.”      The    interview    notes    further   indicate   that   availability,  client   budget,

equipment requirements, and other event conditions can affect the final package and price. This

means    that  the   price  discussed     at  the  beginning     of  an  inquiry   may    not  always be the      final

amount     agreed    upon.    An   online   estimator    can   therefore    be   useful   for  providing    an   initial

reference, but the final price still needs to be discussed directly with the owner.



=== PAGE 13 ===

Payment     arrangements       are   also  part   of  the   booking    process.    The   interview    notes

indicate  that the studio requires a 30% non-refundable down payment and that payment

agreements are      discussed directly with the client. The notes also indicate that there are cases

where an agreed plan may change and adjustments or additional fees may be required. These

findings further show why the booking process cannot rely only on an automatically calculated

price or an automatically confirmed booking. The information recorded during the initial inquiry

must remain open to administrative review until the final arrangement has been discussed and

agreed upon.


         At  the  interpretative,    or  "why,"   level, the difficulty exists because        general   information

and event-specific arrangements are handled through the same communication process. Clients

may   need    to ask the    owner    separately about      portfolio  samples, packages,        prices, schedules,

and  other   service   details   before   the  owner    can move     on   to the   specific requirements of the

event. At the same time, information about the client, schedule, payment agreement, and final

arrangement      must    be   preserved     across    conversations      and   manual     records.    This   creates

repeated explanations and additional work even when some of the information could already be

made    available   before   direct  consultation.    The    owner    confirmed    the   value  of  improving    this

stage by stating, “An online estimator would be a great help because clients could already have

an idea of the possible price before we discuss the final arrangement.”


         The interview findings also indicate that the studio’s need for organized records becomes

more important as       the number      of inquiries   changes throughout the year. The interview notes

describe   the business as seasonal, with fewer clients during the rainy season and more clients

during   the  dry   season.   The   notes   also identify    weddings and birthdays         as common       types   of

events, while burial and christening bookings occur less frequently. These observations do not

consistent booking and client records when the volume and type of inquiries change.

HMW Question and Solution Ideas Considered


         Based   on   the  validated    problem    statement,     the  researchers     used   the How     Might   We

method to     convert   the identified problem into a design opportunity. The purpose of this stage

was not yet to choose a final feature, but to reframe the owner’s difficulties in a way that would

guide   possible   solutions. Since    the existing process       depends    heavily on Facebook Messenger,

phone    calls,  personal    discussion,    handwritten     records,   and    manual    schedule     checking,    the

design   challenge    needed to    focus on both sides of        the workflow:      the client’s need for clearer

information    before    booking    and   the   owner’s    need   for  organized     records   after  receiving    an

inquiry.


         The   resulting   How     Might   We    question    is:  How    might    we   help   prospective     clients

independently review Jonathan Photography’s portfolio, understand available services, prepare a

preliminary    estimate,    and   submit    complete    booking     details,  while   helping   the   administrator

organize    inquiries,    schedules,     and    service    information      without    removing      the   personal

communication needed to finalize a photography booking?


         This question     reflects  the owner’s interview response that direct communication should

still remain   part of  the process. Photography services may              require   adjustments depending on

the event, location, requested coverage, budget, and final agreement. Therefore, the proposed

solution should not replace the owner’s judgment or personal negotiation with clients. Instead,



=== PAGE 17 ===

it should    reduce    repeated    explanations,     give   clients  a  clearer   starting   point,  and   make     the

information easier to review once a client proceeds with a booking request.


         Using   the   HMW     question    as  the   basis   for brainstorming,      the  researchers     considered

several possible solution       ideas. One     idea  was to create      a  categorized portfolio website where

clients could    view  the studio’s previous       work before sending        a message. Another idea was to

provide an online      service and package estimator           that  would allow clients to see a preliminary

price  range   based    on   their  selected    event   type,   coverage    hours,    and   add-ons.    A  structured

online   booking    request    form   was    also   considered     so  that   client  details,  event    information,

preferred    schedule,    and    contact    information     could   be   submitted     in  a  more    complete     and

organized manner.


         For  the   administrator    side, the    researchers    considered     an  administrative dashboard, a

booking    request    tracker,  a  calendar    for  requested     and confirmed schedules,          and  an   editable

service catalogue connected to the public estimator. These ideas directly respond to the owner’s

need to monitor dates, retrieve previous inquiry information, and update service prices without

manually     repeating     the   same    information      across   separate     tools.   Other    ideas    were    also

considered,     such   as   a  Facebook      chatbot,    shared    spreadsheet,      client  account     portal,  SMS

notification, online payment processing, electronic contract, and photo delivery platform.


         After  brainstorming,     the researchers recognized that           not all ideas were appropriate for

the initial  version of   the system. Features         such  as  online payment, electronic contracts, client

accounts,     and    full   photo    delivery     would     require    wider    legal,    financial,   and    storage

considerations.    These    may be useful in future development, but they would expand the scope

beyond the current capstone’s main problem. The ideas most aligned with the interview findings

were the    portfolio   website,   preliminary    estimator,    structured booking request workflow, admin



=== PAGE 18 ===

dashboard,     calendar,   editable   services,   and   centralized    request    management,       because     these

directly address the owner’s current difficulties while preserving the direct client communication

that remains necessary for final agreements.

Top Three Ideas, Scoring, and Justification


         Based    on   the   solution    ideas   generated      from   the   HMW     question,    the   researchers

identified three ideas to carry forward for further comparison. The purpose of this stage was to

consider   different   possible    directions   before   selecting    the  idea   that  would    best  address    the

identified problem. The three ideas were selected based on how they could improve the client’s

access    to  information,     support     the   owner’s    existing   booking     process,    and    organize    the

information    involved in    inquiries and schedules. The project guidelines require the researchers

to identify the    Top 3 solution     ideas   and   evaluate them based        on   user impact,    feasibility, and

business value.


         The   first  idea   was   an   Integrated     Booking     and   Portfolio   Management        System    that

combines the portfolio, service and package information, preliminary price estimator, structured

booking    request   form,   administrative     dashboard,     and   calendar    into  one   system. The second

idea  was a    Centralized Request and Calendar Tracker with a basic inquiry form, which focuses

mainly on organizing client requests, schedules, and booking records on the administrator side.

The  third idea was a Portfolio and Estimator Website with direct communication, where clients

can  view   previous    work,   review    services   and   packages,     and   prepare    a  preliminary    estimate

before continuing through Facebook Messenger, phone, or personal consultation.



=== PAGE 19 ===

These    three  ideas were     selected because       they   represent    different ways of      addressing

the problems identified       during   the interview. The owner explained that clients usually contact

the studio through chat or personal conversations and commonly ask about available packages

and prices. He also stated that schedules and client transactions are currently monitored using a

calendar   and record     book.   In  addition,   he   identified an    online  estimator and more accessible

booking records as useful improvements to the existing process.


         To further compare the three ideas, the researchers plotted them on a 2×2 matrix using

client-facing   depth    and   administrative     record-keeping      depth    as  the   two   dimensions.     These

dimensions were chosen because the identified problem involves both the client and the owner.

Clients need clearer access to services, portfolio information, and preliminary pricing, while the

owner needs a       more   organized     way to manage inquiries, schedules, and booking information.

The matrix therefore allows the researchers to see how much each idea addresses either side of

the existing process.

Final Solution and TELOS Feasibility Analysis


         Based    on   the  scoring   and    comparison     of  the   three   solution   ideas,  the   researchers

selected the    Integrated Booking and Portfolio Management System as the initial Top 1

idea.  The   selected   solution   combines     the  client-facing    and administrative      functions   identified

during the previous stages of the study. The purpose is to give prospective clients a clearer way

to view the studio’s services, packages, portfolio, and estimated prices while giving the owner a

more    organized    way    to  manage      booking    requests,    client  details,  schedules,    and    payment

agreements. This addresses the existing process in which clients commonly inquire through chat

or personal conversations,       while   important    booking    information is maintained        across separate

conversations, a calendar, and record books.


         The   proposed     solution    is  a  Web-Based         Booking      and    Portfolio     Management

System     for Jonathan Photography. Prospective clients                  will be able to browse the studio’s

portfolio, review available services and packages, prepare a preliminary estimate based on their

selected   requirements,      and   submit    a  booking    request    containing    the   necessary    event    and

contact   information.    On   the  administrator     side,  the  owner    will  be  able   to  review   submitted

requests, monitor requested dates, maintain client and transaction records, and update service

information.    The system     will support the     owner’s    existing  practice of    adjusting packages and

prices based on the client’s needs, event requirements, and availability rather than automatically

determining a final arrangement.


         The system will provide a preliminary estimate and a booking request, rather than

a binding    quotation    or  automatic    confirmation.     The   final price,  additional   requirements,     and



=== PAGE 25 ===

payment     agreement     will  still be discussed    directly  with the    client.  This  is consistent    with the

interview    findings   that   pricing   may     be   adjusted    according     to  the   event   and    that   direct

communication       remains    important    for  reaching    the  final  agreement. The system may             record

agreed payment terms, including the owner’s stated 30% non-refundable down payment, but it

will not require online payment as part of the initial solution. The system is therefore intended

to organize    and   support   the booking process while preserving the owner’s role in consultation

and final confirmation.

         After  the functional prototype       was   implemented,      the researchers conducted         an  owner

interview   and   prototype    testing   session   on   September       23.   The   session   was   conducted     to

understand     the  owner’s    experience    with the    existing  process and to      observe    how   the owner

interacted with the proposed prototype. The prototype-testing activities focused on finding and

reviewing   a booking     request, checking the requested date on the calendar, changing a service

price and checking the estimator, changing website content, and acting as a client to create an

estimate    and   submit   a  booking     request.   The   owner     was   allowed    to  perform    the  activities

without   being guided     through    each individual step so that the         researchers     could  observe the

areas  that  were easy to      understand and the        areas   where   assistance    or  clarification  might be

needed.

                             Plate 31. Owner Prototype Testing Session


         The   prototype     testing    produced     several   observations      regarding    the   usability   and

usefulness of the     proposed     system. When asked which features stood out most positively, the

owner   identified   easy    access, the personal portfolio,             and the user-friendly          nature of

the  system.     These    responses     support   the   decision   to make the     studio’s portfolio and      basic

information directly accessible through the website rather than requiring clients to begin with a

message or personal conversation.



=== PAGE 68 ===

The   owner identified     the  main point of      confusion    as the system      not  being sufficiently

friendly    for  someone        who    is  not   computer       literate.    This  feedback     indicates   that  the

prototype should place greater emphasis on simple navigation, clear labels, and straightforward

interactions so that administrative        users   with limited computer experience can use the system

more comfortably.


         The   distinction   between    the estimate,       booking     request,     and confirmed         booking

was reported as      clear.  This  is important because the proposed system is intended to create a

booking    request    rather   than   complete     the   booking     automatically.    The   result   supports    the

decision   to  maintain    a  separate    preliminary-estimation      stage   and   to  leave   final  confirmation

under the owner’s control.


         The   owner   also  indicated that the administrator navigation was understandable.

No   major    missing    feature    was    identified   during    the   feedback     session,   while   the   owner

suggested adding time information to the process. This provides a specific improvement for

the  researchers    to  consider,    particularly in   the booking request and schedule            portions   of the

prototype.


         The    feedback     session    therefore     identified    both    positive   aspects     and    areas    for

improvement.      Easy   access   to the   system,    the presentation      of the  personal    portfolio,  and   the

overall  user-friendly    design    were    recognized     as  strengths.    At  the   same    time,   the  concern

regarding     users   who     are   not    computer     literate   indicates    a   need    for  further    usability

improvements,      while   the  request    for  time   information    provides    a  specific  adjustment     to  the

scheduling-related portion of the interface.


         The interview and prototype testing also support the continued separation between the

preliminary estimate,      booking request,      and   final confirmation.     The   owner’s feedback indicates



=== PAGE 69 ===

that this distinction is understandable within the prototype, while the existing interview findings

establish   that   direct   communication       remains     necessary     for  package     adjustments      and   final

arrangements.      This  keeps    the prototype consistent with         the actual workflow       identified during

the study.


         The   researchers     will  use   the   testing   feedback     as  the   basis  for  the   next   prototype

iteration. The immediate improvements identified are to make the system easier for users with

limited  computer     familiarity  and   to provide    clearer   time information within the scheduling and

booking-request      process.    The   owner’s    overall   response     did  not  identify   an  additional    major

feature   that   needed    to  be   added    at  this  stage,   allowing    the  researchers     to  keep   the   MVP

focused on the validated problem and the defined user flow.


         The wireframes, functional prototype, and owner testing session therefore complete the

progression     from    design     structure    to   implementation        and    stakeholder      evaluation.    The

wireframes    establish   the intended structure of the MVP, the functional prototype demonstrates

the  proposed     workflow     in  practice,   and   the   owner’s    feedback     provides    direct  evidence     for

evaluating the usability and clarity of the current design. The results will be used to refine the

prototype    while   maintaining     the   central   purpose    of  the  project:   providing    clearer   access    to

information    and   more    organized    booking-request and schedule           records without removing the

direct communication required by Jonathan Photography.
